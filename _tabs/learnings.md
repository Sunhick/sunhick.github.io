Lessons I've picked up over the years, mostly the hard way. I come back to this list often and revise it as I learn more.

1. **Nothing is trivial.**
2. **Follow the 80/20 rule.** Spend 80% of the time understanding the problem, until the solution becomes obvious, and 20% building it.
3. **Trust, but always verify**, especially with AI and LLMs. A wrong decision is expensive for you and costs the model nothing.
4. **Meet with stakeholders often and be transparent**, including AppSec, Principal Engineers, and anyone else with a stake in the outcome.
5. **The company chooses a manager; the team chooses a leader.**
6. **Learning is always on the learner, never on the teacher.**
7. **Use AI to learn and improve, not to hand off work you don't understand.** Delegating without learning only makes you dependent on the tool.
8. **Simple beats clever.** If you can delete something and nothing breaks, delete it.
9. **Everything expires.** Credentials, certificates, and keys, but also software, solutions, designs, and documentation. They all need to evolve, so revisit them before they decay.
10. **Disagree early, then commit.** Raise concerns before the decision; once it's made, back it fully.
11. **Measure before you optimize.** Your intuition about where the time goes is usually wrong.
12. **Own the outcome, not the task.** Closing the ticket isn't the goal; solving the customer's problem is.
13. **Fix the system, not the symptom.** When something breaks twice, find out why it was possible at all.
14. **Feedback is a gift.** Ask for it often, and act on it so people keep giving it.
